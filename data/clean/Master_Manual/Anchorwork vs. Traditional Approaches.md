CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) • Focus: Thoughts drive feelings; change the thought to change the emotion. • Tools: Thought records, cognitive distortions, logic-based reframes. • Limits: Often bypasses the body, relational triggers, or attachment wounds. Anchorwork Difference: • Doesn’t debate your storm — it honors it, names it, and offers breath-based Stewardship instead of correction. 2. DBT (Dialectical Behavioral Therapy) • Focus: Emotions are valid, but behaviors need regulation. • Tools: Distress tolerance, mindfulness, radical acceptance, emotion labeling. • Limits: Strong focus on skill performance; can feel scripted, especially during real- time triggers. Anchorwork Difference: • Offers relational rituals instead of scripts. • Doesn’t just say “accept” — it gives you breath, metaphors, and return plans. • It replaces “What skill do I apply now?” with “What ritual helps us re-anchor right now?” 3. IFS (Internal Family Systems) • Focus: Healing inner parts (protector, exile, manager, etc.) through self- compassion. • Tools: Inner dialogue, part mapping, unburdening. • Limits: Beautiful internally, but can be hard to use with another person in real-time. Anchorwork Difference: • Less internal mapping, more external emotional navigation. • Ideal for relational trauma, co-regulation, storm seasons in couples. • “It’s not your exile reacting. It’s your nervous system in a storm. Let’s anchor together.” 4. Somatic Therapy / SE (Somatic Experiencing) • Focus: Body holds trauma. Healing through physical awareness. • Tools: Titration, pendulation, grounding, sensation tracking. • Limits: Can be abstract; lacks clear verbal systems for relational healing. Anchorwork Difference: • Honors the body with story, structure, and metaphor. • Body-based yes — but also relational, ritualized, language-accessible. • “Let’s drop anchor” becomes a real nervous system act — not just an idea. ⚓ What Anchorwork Adds (That Others Rarely Do): Anchorwork Contribution Why It Changes the Game Metaphors that feel alive (ship, anchor, storm, breath, compass) They give people usable language in the middle of overwhelm Real-time couple rituals Helps people navigate when one is shut down and the other is flooding — now, not just in theory Shared emotional language Stops the “I don’t know how to explain what I feel” loop ⚓ In Plain Terms: CBT helps you change how you think. DBT helps you survive emotional distress. IFS helps you make peace with your inner parts. Somatic therapy helps you feel what your body holds. Anchorwork helps you and the people you love: • Understand your storms • Stay present through pain • Speak your needs without shame • Repair what you never thought could be healed • And build emotional ecosystems that are sustainable, relational, and sacred. It’s not here to replace the others. It’s here to complete what they couldn’t hold — the space between Ships, the words when your nervous system goes silent, the breath that says “I still care” even when you’re shut down.
