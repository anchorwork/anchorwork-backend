Title: Anchorwork™ and Polyvagal Theory: The Evolution of Theory Author: Rebecca Morris Affiliation: Independent Researcher, Founder of Anchorwork™ Email: stewardship@theanchorwork.com Word Count: 204 Abstract: This poster introduces Anchorwork™, a narrative-based, trauma-informed emotional framework that contributes to the ongoing evolution of Polyvagal Theory. Grounded in lived experience, somatic research, and collective grief practice, Anchorwork™ offers symbolic structure for individuals navigating trauma, neurodivergence, and intergenerational legacy. Core concepts from Polyvagal Theory are translated into metaphoric vessels—Anchor (ventral vagal regulation), Hull (somatic safety), Storm (sympathetic arousal), and Stillwater (parasympathetic return)—creating an accessible emotional map that avoids pathologization. Sensory-aligned rituals such as Anchor Drop, Weather Forecasting, and Crew Council support nervous system regulation through consent-based pacing, story, and embodied rhythm. This framework responds directly to critiques of Polyvagal Theory by: ➢ Providing a culturally fluent, non-diagnostic structure for emotional regulation. ➢ Centering emotional biodiversity, legacy grief, and symbolic differentiation. ➢ Offering qualitative coherence markers in place of overreliance on HRV as a singular indicator. Rather than reducing vagal theory to behavioral control or mechanistic response, Anchorwork reframes the nervous system as a narrative system—responsive to ritual, relationship, and return. It positions regulation not as compliance, but as reconnection. This poster invites clinicians, educators, and cultural stewards to explore how symbolic nervous system mapping, when rooted in consent and complexity, can expand the clinical and cultural reach of polyvagal-aligned care.
