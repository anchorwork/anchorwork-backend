Meet the Family: The Heart Behind The Anchorwork Method Rebecca is the heart and creator of The Anchorwork Methoda deeply personal and emotionally intelligent healing system born from her lived experience as a trauma survivor, mother, wife, creative, and neurodivergent woman. Adopted at two days old, she was raised in a highly structured and emotionally complex household marked by loss, religious rigidity, and a lifelong struggle for belonging and understanding. Her childhood was defined by emotional neglect, high expectations, and the lasting grief of a mother mourning another child. Despite this, Rebecca emerged as a fiercely empathetic, hyper-intelligent, passionate person with extraordinary sensory awareness and creative gifts. She is a professional baker, writer, musician, and former jazz vocalist, and shes especially known for her exquisite cakes and ability to create beauty under pressure. Diagnosed with ADHD in 2023 after years of misdiagnoses, Rebecca finally found tools that helped her thrivebut still faces lingering trauma responses and emotional dysregulation that she continues to navigate and grow through. Rebecca is a mother to three daughters and married to Rick, with whom she has built a life of simplicity, creativity, struggle, and fierce devotion. She is the voice many wish they had as children. Her deepest goal is to break generational trauma and create emotionally safe, radically loving environments for her family and others. Three words to describe Rebeccas heart: warm, big, heavy. Rick is Rebeccas husband and the quiet strength behind The Anchorwork Method. He is intelligent, introverted, and thoughtfuloften expressing love through actions more than words. With a background in management and a bachelors degree in University Studies, Rick brings a calm, steady presence to a household full of energy, creativity, and emotion. He grew up with limited emotional expression in a home shaped by divorce and quiet resignation. His father was a military man who was emotionally distant, and his mother worked hard to support the family as a single parent. Though Rick doesnt describe his childhood as traumatic, he has struggled with expressing feelings, initiating connection, and building deep emotional intimacyespecially after years of defaulting to passivity as a coping mechanism. Despite personal challenges, Rick has loved and cared for Rebeccas daughters as his own. He is a loyal, supportive partner who is on a path of rediscovering his own emotional language, purpose, and presence. Three words to describe Ricks heart: quiet, loyal, tender. Anneliese (Anna) is Rebeccas firstborn, born in 2011. She is intellectually gifted, funny, and self-assured, with a strong bond to her biological father and a deep sense of self. Diagnosed with ADHD, she is sharp, independent, and emotionally complexnavigating adolescence with curiosity and strength. She lives part-time with each parent. Juliet, born in 2016, is energetic, talkative, and deeply empathetic. She shares many of Rebeccas traits, including emotional sensitivity and creative thinking. Juliet often struggles with overstimulation and regulation, but she is deeply affectionate and always seeking connection. She challenges and inspires Rebecca daily. Ella, born in 2020, is autistic and Rebecca and Ricks youngest daughter together. She communicates her needs nonverbally with clarity and presence. Her world is rich in sensory experience, and she finds deep comfort laying beside Rebecca. She reminds the family that connection doesnt always require words. These three girls are the reason behind the method. They are the hope and the healing. Their joy, challenges, and unique needs have shaped every tool in this toolkit. Three words to describe their bond: spirited, loud, fierce.
