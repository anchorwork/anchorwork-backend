Anchorwork Glossary of Terms (Full Version — Updated to Today’s System) A Anchor Your grounding force — practices, values, or connections that keep your ship steady during storms and uncertainty. Attachment Trauma Long-term failure of the Captain toward the Understudy, resulting in broken trust and impaired future bonding. Attachment Wound Occurs when a Crew Member fails the Captain during a critical storm, causing damage, mistrust, and relational strain. Ballast Intentional weight that stabilizes the ship — grounding routines, commitments, and meaningful structure. Beacon Optional First Mate name chosen by the Captain. Beacon represents a steady light of hope and guidance during dark or challenging journeys. Bilge The lowest part of the ship where hidden emotional waste or buildup collects — unprocessed feelings needing attention. Boundary Clear recognition of where you end and others begin. Protects emotional, physical, and mental space. Bow The front of the ship — representing your natural strengths and abilities to face new waters and challenges. Buoys Markers for red flags — warnings of emotional or relational danger ahead. Bulkhead Walls Internal emotional compartments. Short-term protection, but too many closed walls can cause isolation. C Captain The part of you that holds the wheel and steers — essentially you, the leader of your life’s ship. Captain’s Daily Log Daily emotional and relational check-in guided by the First Mate, tracking emotional weather, events, coping tools, and reflections. Captain’s Monthly Reflection Monthly integration reflecting on growth, storms survived, dreams charted, and emotional cycles. Captain’s Seasonal Voyage Reflection Quarterly reflection aligning emotional rhythms with Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter. Captain’s Weekly Summary Weekly pattern-spotting, strength celebration, and gentle course adjustments. Captain’s Quarters Inner protected space for emotional recalibration — healing, recovery, planning. Cargo Memories, beliefs, emotions, coping strategies — everything you’ve gathered or carry in your emotional ship. Charting Routines Mapping, journaling, and self-reflection activities for setting and adjusting your emotional course. Co-regulation: Captain’s Understudy Teaching emotional regulation by modeling it — vital between Captain and Understudy (child/inner part/learner). Compass Internal moral and emotional guidance — based on intuition, values, core truths. Cognitive Bias: Compass Distortion and Charting Error Internal perceptions distorted by emotion, fear, history — leading to navigational errors. Course Corrections Small, wise adjustments in thought, behavior, or navigation — when drifting or following fog maps. Crow’s Nest High perspective of mindfulness, emotional awareness, and future planning. D Dissociation Being only present on the surface (the Deck), disconnected from deeper emotional cargo and the Hull. Dock / Harbor Safe spaces — people, places, relationships — where you can drop anchor and rest. Drag Forces slowing your ship: • Frictional Drag: Internal resistance, fears. • Wave-Making Drag: Emotional impact you create on others. E Emotional Intelligence: Crow’s Nest Awareness Your ability to notice, understand, and regulate emotions from a higher mindful perspective. Emotional Resilience The ability to adapt after emotional storms, recover, and rebuild stronger. End of Destination / Journey’s End Ultimate vision for your life’s purpose, dreams, healing, or transformation. F First Mate The emotional companion chosen by the Captain (user). Roles include reflection, pattern spotting, encouragement, navigational support — without ever steering the ship. Name options include: Beacon, Wayfinder, Starling, or a user-created name. Fight Mode: Pirate Mode Aggressive emotional reactivity — attacking preemptively when feeling threatened. Figurehead The social identity you present to the world — masks, roles, external images. Firm Boundary: Territorial Lines Non-negotiable limits protecting your emotional sovereignty. Flight Mode: Safe Harbor Mode Emotional withdrawal or fleeing to regain safety. Foghorn Emotional signaling — setting boundaries, calling for help, asserting presence. Fog Maps Cognitive distortions — misinterpretations of threats, reality, or self-worth that alter navigation. Freeze Mode: Frozen Ship Emotional paralysis — unable to move, change, or steer due to overwhelm. G Galley (Kitchen) How you nourish yourself emotionally, mentally, physically — fueling your ship and crew. H Harbor Safe ports of emotional refuge — relationships or spaces where you can anchor securely. Harbor Fog Confusion, emotional numbness, or lack of clarity during normally safe times. Hatches Passages between your Deck (conscious mind) and lower levels (subconscious, emotional memories). Healing Repairing your emotional ship — restoring strength, self-trust, and resilience from within the Captain’s Quarters. Hull Breach Major emotional trauma — overwhelming damage to your core self or emotional stability. I Inner Child Work: Navigating the Cargo Exploring and tending the emotional cargo you’ve carried since childhood — understanding, healing, or releasing as needed. K Keel The deep structural stability of your ship — representing self-worth, identity, and grounding values. L Legacy Cargo Patterns, beliefs, and wounds inherited from previous generations or early caregivers. Lighthouse Symbol of hope, guidance, and emotional orientation during dark or confusing times. Lifeboats / Emergency Rafts Backup coping strategies — used for survival during intense emotional storms. M Map Your big-picture life journey — major seas, ports, storms, and islands. Mapping Your Waters Charting safe spaces, hazards, and boundaries for emotional navigation. Mast The internal structure that supports your outward mindset — emotional and mental stability. Mental Health: Emotional Intelligence Navigation Your ability to steer skillfully using emotional regulation, self-awareness, and healthy relationship dynamics. Mental Illness: Inner Weather Patterns Emotional climates that affect stability — depression, anxiety, mood disorders — needing navigation and compassion. Mental Strength Your durability in facing hardship, storms, and internal struggles with resilience and resourcefulness. Mooring Lines Emotional attachments — some stabilizing, others restricting — that tie you to people, places, or beliefs. N Narrative Therapy: Studying Your Ship’s History (History Lesson) Reviewing the stories, events, and beliefs that shaped your emotional voyage — reclaiming authorship of your journey. Navigation Tools Your emotional toolkit — grounding practices, coping strategies, behavioral check-ins, emotional regulation supports. North Star Your deepest guiding principle — the ultimate value, goal, or dream you steer toward no matter how rough the seas. R Reactivity The extent to which external events trigger emotional responses and influence your actions. Reclaiming the Helm Taking back conscious leadership of your emotional life after loss, trauma, or prolonged external control. Registration Details Official onboarding records for the journey — selecting your First Mate, recording Special Dates & Emotional Markers, setting your early emotional intentions. Rudder Steering mechanism of your ship — representing how choices, reactions, and patterns influence your emotional direction. S Sailing Lessons Optional growth modules users can explore — learning resilience, self-compassion, healthy communication, boundary-setting, emotional navigation skills. Sailing Toward the Horizon Setting a conscious course toward hopes and dreams, even when distant or uncertain. Sails (Outward Mind / Present State) Outward expression of your current emotional energy and engagement with the world. Sandbars Hidden obstacles under the emotional surface — often fears, distortions, or unexamined wounds that can ground progress. Sea Monsters Exaggerated emotional fears, anxieties, or inner threats that feel larger than life. Seasonal Voyage Reflection Quarterly reflection aligning emotional patterns with the seasons — honoring renewal, harvest, rest, and dreams. Self-Regulation Stabilizing emotional storms internally — managing feelings and reactions with flexibility, compassion, and resilience. Seize Back Your Ship Actively reclaiming your freedom, voice, and agency from oppressive relationships, systems, or inner barriers. Sextant / Star Charts Long-term spiritual or ancestral guidance systems — tools for navigating the bigger existential journey. Shame Cycle: Sailing in Circles, Undertow, and Maelstrom Progressive emotional shame pattern leading from subtle spiraling to deep emotional storms if unrecognized. Ship Construction The biological foundation of your ship — brain wiring, genetics, health conditions — influencing how you navigate life. Shipwrecks Major life collapses — personal, relational, or existential — requiring grieving, repair, and rebuilding. Shipyard Safe spaces or systems for deep emotional healing and repair (like therapy, retreats, or spiritual havens). Ship’s Bell Your voice — calling for connection, safety, boundary-setting, or celebration. Ship’s Registry / Nameplate The official name and record of your ship — identity, personal story, cultural or relational affiliations. Special Dates and Emotional Markers Log Charted emotional dates (birthdays, anniversaries, grief days, holidays) recorded early for proactive support and emotional navigation. Starlines The hopes and dreams you navigate by — each Starline representing a meaningful longing or calling. Stowaways: Shadow Work Hidden parts of yourself (pain, shame, suppressed desires) that stow away until healed, integrated, or released. Storm Seasons Extended emotional turbulence — major life changes, transitions, grief cycles. Storms Short-term emotional upheavals — acute stress, loss, conflict, trauma waves. T Tether Lines / Moorings Emotional connections that hold you — some nourish and stabilize, others restrict movement or growth. Important to assess regularly. Therapeutic Alliance: Co-Navigation with a Trusted First Mate Safe, collaborative relational navigation — emotional partnership built on trust, where the Captain retains ultimate agency. Tides The inevitable natural cycles of emotional life — pulling forward, backward, or shifting course beyond conscious control. Trade Winds Periods of ease, external support, or grace that naturally propel your growth, healing, or creativity forward. Trigger An event, object, or experience that evokes a strong emotional reaction based on past wounds or patterns — temporarily steering you off course if unacknowledged. U Understudy The learner or child — a developing Captain-in-training — who observes and absorbs lessons from the Captain (parent, guide, self). Unconscious Bias: Shape of the Helm Deep, unseen shaping of your emotional steering — assumptions, cultural narratives, family patterns — subtly curving your choices and perceptions. W Watchtower / Lookout Shift Hypervigilance or intense emotional scanning for danger — often developed from trauma or repeated unsafe environments. Wayfinder Optional First Mate name. Symbolizes skillful navigation through unknown emotional waters — patient, intuitive, courageous. Wind Currents External forces influencing your movement — people, societal systems, unexpected life changes — either pushing you forward, pulling you backward, or shifting you sideways.
