Anchorwork Onboarding Journey – Expanded Page-by-Page Overview Start Screen: Welcome Aboard Purpose: Introduce Anchorwork’s metaphor and emotional tone. Establish that this is a journey, not a fix. Highlights: • Header: This voyage is yours. You are the Captain. • Subtext: We’re here to help you drop anchor when waters rise, and set sail when you’re ready to move forward with purpose. • Rope-bordered “LET’S SET SAIL” button • Branded layout with anchor emblem and nautical wave footer Page 1: Who’s Setting Sail? Purpose: Determine the user’s role and intention for entering Anchorwork (individual, couple, caregiver, etc.) Highlights: • Button-style choices: Individual, Couple, Co-parent, Caregiver, Educator, Just Exploring • Sets the foundation for personalizing their experience from the start Page 2: Captain’s Profile Purpose: Gathers basic user identity information in a safe, human way. Fields: • Name (“What should the Captain be called?”) • Pronouns (dropdown with custom option) • Age range (dropdown) • Consent note: You can update or remove this information at any time in your settings. Page 3: Who’s in Your Crew? Purpose: Understand the user’s living situation and relational context. Fields: • Living situation dropdown • Slider: “How steady does your crew feel right now?” (from Adrift to Steady & Strong) • Open text: “Want to tell us more about your crew?” • Consent note: You can update or remove this information anytime in your settings. Page 4: Identifying Inner Weather Patterns Purpose: Gently uncover emotional history without using diagnostic labels. Fields: • Multi-select dropdown: “What waves have you sailed through?” (e.g., grief, anxiety, hurt, loss) • Open text: “Feel free to share any of your storms here…” • Consent note: You are not your waves. You are the Captain, still steering. Page 5: Charting the Map Purpose: Help the user reflect on where they currently are in their emotional life. Fields: • Multi-select dropdown: “What kind of waters are you navigating?” (e.g., loss, identity shifts, overwhelm) • Open text: “Describe your waters in your own words…” • Consent note: You’re allowed to name the storm—or sail quietly through it. Page 6: Star Charts & Horizons Purpose: Identify hopes, dreams, or internal goals the user is reaching for. Fields: • Multi-select dropdown: “What are you sailing toward?” (e.g., peace, clarity, creativity, reconnection) • Open text: “Tell us more about what you’re sailing toward…” • Consent note: Just as stars move across the sky, so can your goals. Page 7: Construct Your Ship Purpose: Help the user explore the strengths and vulnerabilities of their current inner structure — including brain, biology, and experience. Subheader: Every ship is built from a framework: your genetics, your biology, your brain’s design, and the waters you’ve sailed through. Anchorwork helps you understand your vessel, strengthen it where needed, and adapt with skill and care as you chart your journey. Fields: • Dropdown: “What parts of your ship already feel strong?” • Dropdown: “Where might your ship need extra support?” • Open text: “Describe anything else about your ship…” • Consent note: Ships evolve. Materials can strengthen. New blueprints can be drawn. You are not limited by where you began. Page 8: Set Your Compass Purpose: Begin to identify the internal and external forces shaping the user’s current sense of direction. Subheader: A compass shows where your ship is pointed, guided by internal guides and swayed by external forces. Anchorwork helps you notice what’s influencing you—so you can choose your course more consciously. You’ll explore this more deeply as your journey continues. Fields: • Dropdown: “What’s informing your compass today?” • Open text: “Describe any other pulls on your compass…” • Consent note: You can update your responses anytime as your voyage unfolds.
