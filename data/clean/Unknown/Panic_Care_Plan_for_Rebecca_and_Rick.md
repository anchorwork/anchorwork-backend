Panic Care Plan for Rebecca This plan is for moments when Rebecca wakes up in a panic or becomes emotionally overwhelmed. It is designed to help Rick respond with compassion, clarity, and structure—especially when words are hard to find. The goal is not to fix the panic, but to stay connected through it. What’s Happening During a Panic Attack Rebecca’s nervous system is in survival mode. Her brain cannot access logic or emotional regulation. This is not a conscious choice. Her words may come out sharp, defensive, or panicked. What she needs most is grounding, not argument or withdrawal. What NOT to Do - Do not get defensive.- Do not argue back.- Do not shut down or walk away without telling her you’ll return.- Do not try to rationalize or fix her panic.- Do not interpret her words as personal attacks. Immediate Actions Rick Can Take - Speak in a calm, gentle tone.- Make physical contact if safe (place hand on her back or hold her hand).- Turn on a light or offer her grounding tools (cold water, lavender oil, weighted pillow).- Sit up with her, even if you’re tired—your presence helps regulate her nervous system. Grounding Phrases Rick Can Say - “I’m here. I’m not going anywhere.”- “You’re not in danger. This is panic. It will pass.”- “You’re safe right now. I’ve got you.”- “I love you. You don’t have to go through this alone.”- “You don’t have to explain—just breathe. We’ll get through it.”- “Let’s breathe together. In… out… in… out…”- “Your feelings make sense. I’m not mad at you.” If She’s Lashing Out Verbally - “I hear that you’re scared. I’m not your enemy.”- “Let’s pause—this is panic, not the truth.”- “You’re safe, even if it doesn’t feel like it. I’m not going anywhere.”- “I’m with you, even if your fear is louder than me right now.” After the Panic Subsides - Offer a hug or continue soft touch if welcomed.- Say: “You’re okay. You’re still loved. That was hard, and I’m proud of you.”- Ask gently: “Do you want to talk about it now, or just be close?”- Validate again: “It makes sense why that happened. You’re not broken.” Daily Prevention & Connection - Create a gentle bedtime ritual: calming music, affirmations, and shared moments.- Reduce stress build-up with daytime emotional check-ins.- Rick: ask “Is there something weighing on you today?” to help Rebecca feel seen.- Rebecca: journal or brain-dump racing thoughts before bed.- Practice being teammates, not opponents—even during hard moments.
