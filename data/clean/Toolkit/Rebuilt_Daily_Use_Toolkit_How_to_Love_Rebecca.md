How to Love Rebecca in Real Time: Complete Daily Use Toolkit for Rick 1. When She’s Triggered, Panicked, or Spiraling - Use a soft tone. Silence feels like abandonment. Stay close. - Offer: blanket, room-temp Dr. Pepper, cats, soft light, jazz. - Say: 'You’re not too much. I’m here with you, no matter what.' - Ask: 'Do you want touch, silence, music, or me just sitting with you?' - Avoid problem-solving or defensiveness. Presence is the medicine. - Mirror her breath, sit on the ground with her, slow everything down. 2. How to Reconnect After a Fight - Say: 'I know you’re hurting. I still love you. Let’s find our way back.' - Even if you don’t understand everything, say: 'I care enough to learn.' - Small gestures matter: bring a comfort item, text something kind, initiate a hug. - Let her cry, let her talk. Don’t jump in with logic. Just listen. - If she storms off, wait 10 minutes and gently follow up. Don’t wait for her to fix it. 3. How to Make Her Feel Loved Without Being Asked - Leave notes: 'You’re so strong and soft at the same time. I love that about you.' - Start dinner without being asked. Offer a blanket or playlist. - Light a candle she loves and say: 'Thought this would feel nice.' - Ask: 'Is there something that would help your heart today?' - Surprise her with something in her aesthetic—floral, cat-themed, or music-based. 4. Grounding Rebecca Through Sensory Regulation - Her top calming scents: Rose Noir & Oud, Driftwood, Sea Salt. - Offer weighted blanket, play The Piano Guys, keep space dim and quiet. - Never touch without asking during panic. Say: 'Do you want to be held?' - Let her hold something cold, or press her palms to a surface to ground. 5. Intimacy & Emotional Connection - Say: 'You’re beautiful when you take control. I love that version of you.' - Don’t shy away from her dominance—it’s how she feels safest and most expressive. - Ask her what feels good. Affirm her body. Say her name when she’s in charge. - Don’t disappear after intimacy. Stay present. Touch her hair. Speak softly. 6. Morning, Midday, and Evening Support - Morning: Greet her with warmth or coffee. 'You’ve got this. I believe in you.' - Midday: Check in via text—ask how her brain and body are holding up. - Evening: Offer rituals—music, candles, shared playlists, calm re-entry after chaos. 7. When She’s Silent or Withdrawn - Silence from her means she feels dismissed or misunderstood. - Sit near. Don’t force her to talk, but say: 'I’m here when you’re ready. I love you even in silence.' - Show care without needing conversation—run a bath, play music, light candles. 8. Supporting Her During PMS, Illness, or Exhaustion - Lower all stimulation. Offer soft foods, warm drinks, no questions. - Ask: 'What do you need least right now?' then remove that thing. - Let her rest and feel safe. Whisper. Use gentle gestures instead of words. 9. Encouraging Her When She Feels Like a Failure - Say: 'You don’t have to earn your worth. It’s already there.' - 'You don’t have to do everything. You’re already enough.' - Remind her of her impact. Reflect specific things she’s done with care. 10. Co-Regulation and High Emotion Moments - Match your breathing to hers. Sit with her, shoulder-to-shoulder. - Use grounding phrases: 'We’re here. We’re okay. You’re not alone.' - Offer physical grounding only with consent. If she says yes, press your palm to hers and breathe together.
