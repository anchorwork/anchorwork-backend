Rebecca & Rick: Family & Parenting Guide for a Neurodivergent Home This guide is for the family you’re building—where ADHD, autism, trauma, and love all live side by side. It includes practical care, emotional co-regulation, daily reflection, and generational healing practices. 1. Daily Parenting Prompts (One Shared Question Each Day) • What did one of our kids teach us today—about themselves or about us? • When did we co-regulate together instead of react? • What’s one small moment of connection we created today? • Did we model love during conflict—or just survival? • How can we help each daughter feel seen this week? 2. Supporting Our Neurodivergent Daughters (ADHD + Autism) • Narrate emotion out loud: 'You’re not in trouble. You’re just feeling frustrated. That’s okay.' • Keep language simple, sensory-friendly, and slow-paced. • Validate without fixing. Allow space for overwhelm without rushing to solutions. • Create consistent rituals (music, scents, playlists, safe space signals). • Know the difference between defiance and dysregulation. 3. What Our Daughters Need From Us • Safety—even when they meltdown. • Consistency—especially when we feel inconsistent inside. • Regulation—not perfection. Our breath regulates theirs. • Repair—modeling 'I was wrong. Let me try again.' • Grace. Lots of it. For them, and for ourselves. 4. What to Avoid With Neurodivergent Kids • Shame-based parenting ('Why would you do that?'). • Overexplaining when they’re already overwhelmed. • Dismissing sensory overload as misbehavior. • Expecting neurotypical performance in neurodivergent bodies. 5. How Our Marriage Models Safety for Them • Every repair between us shows them love isn’t about perfection. • How we handle conflict becomes the voice in their heads. • Our teamwork teaches them trust. Our shared rituals teach them presence. • The way Rick supports Rebecca teaches them that gentle men exist. • The way Rebecca honors Rick’s quiet teaches them not all love is loud. 6. How We Break Generational Trauma • We say what wasn’t said: 'You are enough.' 'I see you.' • We name emotions in real-time. • We pause instead of punish. • We co-regulate instead of dominate. • We repair instead of retreat. 7. Recommended Resources for Parenting with Purpose • 'The Explosive Child' by Ross Greene • 'Whole-Brain Child' by Dan Siegel & Tina Payne Bryson • 'Self-Reg' by Stuart Shanker • 'Unconditional Parenting' by Alfie Kohn • Dr. Mona Delahooke’s books on neurodivergence and co-regulation • Podcasts: Tilt Parenting, The Autism Helper, ADHD Essentials
