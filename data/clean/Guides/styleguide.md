Anchorwork™ Scientific Style Guide (for use in all whitepapers, papers, formal publications) This guide will ensure: Every Anchorwork publication sounds consistent. It stays credible for scientific and clinical audiences. It retains Anchorwork’s sacred, human, hopeful tone. It bridges emotional stewardship and professional science seamlessly. ANCHORWORK™ SCIENTIFIC STYLE GUIDE 1. Tone and Voice Element Guideline Primary Tone Professional, precise, grounded in scientific frameworks. Secondary Tone Warm, human, compassionate. (Especially when discussing emotional experiences, relational healing, or sacred stewardship.) Emotional Tone Respectful, optimistic, never clinical-cold. Avoid excessive technical jargon when unnecessary. Sacred Language Allowed selectively. “Sacred stewardship,” “emotional lighthouse,” “anchor in storms,” and “sacred tending” metaphors are permitted but should be clearly framed as philosophical grounding, not scientific mechanisms. Metaphors Used intentionally as navigational frameworks to aid understanding (ship, ecosystem, lighthouse). Metaphors must be clearly introduced and linked to scientific concepts (emotional regulation, systems theory, attachment theory). 2. Structure of Documents Section Requirements Title Professional, concise (<15 words), clear. Evocative but grounded. Abstract 150–250 words. Clear overview of problem, solution, model/theory, and applications. Introduction Frame the emotional or clinical need for Anchorwork models. Cite existing gaps in emotional education or regulation science. Theoretical Foundations Always grounded in peer-reviewed psychology, neuroscience, attachment theory, trauma theory, systems theory. Cite APA 7th edition sources. Model or Theory Description Clear, structured (e.g., layers of Emotional Ecosystem, ANCHOR steps). Use professional subheadings. Include metaphors framed as frameworks. Applications/Methods Discuss use in therapy, coaching, education, relationships, leadership. Use clear real-world examples. Comparative Analysis Compare Anchorwork gently but confidently to existing models (e.g., emotional regulation models, attachment repair models). Limitations/Future Research Acknowledge where empirical validation is ongoing. Suggest future studies, pilot programs, clinical trials, or case study research. Conclusion Summarize model’s significance and call for emotional stewardship revolution. End with hope, science, and invitation. References APA 7th edition style. Include high-quality peer-reviewed journals, landmark books, key thinkers (e.g., Bowlby, Porges, Siegel, Goleman, Frankl). Include your own works if Anchorwork gets cited across whitepapers. 3. Scientific Language Conventions Element Guideline Voice (Active vs Passive) Primarily active voice for clarity. Passive voice allowed when describing broader scientific consensus (e.g., “It is widely accepted that…”). Person Third person preferred (“Anchorwork proposes…”) unless narratively helpful to use “we” (e.g., “We propose a new emotional stewardship framework…”). Avoid “I” voice except in dedicated founder’s forewords. Sentence Length Clear, moderate length. Vary short and long sentences to maintain engagement without sounding choppy. Jargon Minimize when possible. If technical terms (e.g., neuroception, polyvagal theory, co-regulation) are used, define them briefly when first mentioned. Gender Language Gender-neutral unless context requires specific gender discussion (e.g., wife-husband manual). Use “they” when appropriate. Emotion Language Emotions are treated as valid, universal, biologically and relationally important. No pathologizing (“bad emotions,” “negative behaviors”). Anchorwork views emotions as weather, not flaws. 4.
