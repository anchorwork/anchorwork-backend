Kids & Family Edition: Parenting With Presence, Healing, and Neurodivergent Awareness A parenting guide rooted in emotional literacy, co-regulation, and cycle-breaking care—for your daughters, and for yourselves. 1. Daily Parenting Prompts (for Couples Journal) • What did we model emotionally for our kids today? • What overwhelmed one of our kids today—and how did we respond? • How can we give each child a moment of connection tomorrow? • What wound from our own childhood got triggered in parenting today? • Where did we succeed in being the parents we wish we had? 2. Core Needs of Neurodivergent Children (ADHD & Autism) • Predictable structure—without harsh rigidity. • Validation of their inner experience, even if it doesn’t make sense to us. • Sensory awareness: noise, textures, clothing tags, brightness, overstimulation. • Emotional translation: help naming what they feel before behavior spirals. • Moments of pure connection: shared joy, silliness, safe physical contact. 3. What Neurodivergent Kids Don’t Need • Forced eye contact or mandatory affection. • Shame for stimming, shutdowns, meltdowns. • Constant correction or critique without affirmation. • Being labeled as 'too sensitive' or 'difficult.' • Expectations that ignore emotional or sensory bandwidth. 4. How Our Marriage Models Safety for Our Children • Let them see repair—not just arguments. • Apologize in front of them. Let them witness reconciliation. • Show them we hold each other with grace when one of us is struggling. • Speak to each other with warmth even when frustrated. • Create rituals they can count on—Saturday pancakes, playlist car rides, evening cuddles. 5. Breaking Cycles of Trauma • Replace yelling with naming emotions: 'I feel frustrated, not at you, but at the situation.' • Give choices instead of commands: 'Do you want to clean up with me or on your own?' • Validate tears: 'It’s okay to cry. Let’s sit together until you’re ready.' • Heal yourself alongside them—let them see growth is ongoing and safe. 6. Book & Resource Recommendations • 'The Explosive Child' by Ross W. Greene • 'Unconditional Parenting' by Alfie Kohn • 'The Whole-Brain Child' by Dan Siegel • 'Brain-Body Parenting' by Mona Delahooke • YouTube: Mona Delahooke, Dr. Becky Kennedy, How to ADHD, The Mom Psychologist • Podcasts: Tilt Parenting, Peace and Parenting, Raising Good Humans 7. Anchoring Parenting Practices to Try Together • Co-regulation breathing with a child in distress. • Weekly one-on-one time with each child (15–30 minutes) with no agenda. • Writing love notes or affirmations on mirrors, lunchboxes, or pillows. • Asking bedtime questions like: 'What was your favorite part of the day?' or 'What do you wish grownups understood?'
